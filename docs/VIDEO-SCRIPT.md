## Word-for-word narration

A reservation is a promise: a place, a time, and agreed terms. If a restaurant table becomes unusable before dinner, keeping that promise can mean changing several bookings together. This is Tablekeeper: keep the promise, even when the floor changes.

Behind it is Proofline, a software factory in BAND Desktop. Shivam Gupta directed the product and configured four coding-agent seats: Coordinator, Engineer, Experience and Verifier. The agents wrote and checked the implementation. Their standing mandates describe reusable responsibilities. Restaurant requirements belong in the task.

This is the real production room. One dispatch covered four stages. Engineer and Experience shared implementation work, while Verifier checked committed revisions independently. The handoffs connect decisions to the code and evidence they produced.

That separation caught something the published checks missed. In stage three, a corrupted restore could erase the permanent exception on an individually changed recurring booking. Verifier rejected the candidate. The Engineer seat fixed the history validation. Independent checks then passed, including the original regression. The failed review and fix remain inspectable.

The run also needed operator help after a daemon interruption. The same BAND seats restarted in the same room and acquired new provider sessions. No new task or implementation hints were sent. This was a single-dispatch run, with recovery assistance.

Now look at the manager preview. These are three synthetic bookings at The Orangery. Closing Window nook proposes one move to Garden table, with zero unused seats. The other bookings stay put. Guests keep their arrival times, party sizes and accepted terms. Nothing has moved yet. The manager reviews before applying the plan.

The planner supports up to six tables, four declared pairs and six considered bookings. Every confirmed booking overlapping the closure interval counts. A stale preview must fail safely, and an impossible plan cannot silently cancel a guest.

All four original stages passed their required published checks in both isolated and fresh-clone isolated runs. Stage four passed one hundred and fifty-eight checks. Independent verification also passed sixty-nine HTTP checks. These are finite tests, not production certification.

The hosted companion is separate work from that graded run. The first customer hypothesis is small venues with direct or recurring groups. Seventy-nine dollars per location per month is a price to test, not revenue. Tablekeeper keeps guest promises. Proofline makes the work behind those promises inspectable.
